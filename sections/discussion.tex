\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Analysis}

Despite the small number of participants we decided to test our findings for statistical significance.
We performed pair-wise t-tests on normalized WPM data. We examined all available pairs of points with different latencies belonging to the same participant. Within our dataset, the p-value for the pair-wise t-test was 0.0003, which is below the significance threshold of 0.05, but should still not be considered statistically significant due to the small size of the dataset and interdependency of the pairs of points taken from the same participant.

Nevertheless, we can still assume that the results of a non-pilot experiment would be aligned with our expectations and can provide a degree of justification for a bigger study with more participants.

Since participants were not interviewed, our qualitative findings are restricted to the 5-point scale self-reporting metric. Looking at the plots in figure 2, we can assume that above 100ms users' perceived comfort level is extremely low.
\subsection{Limitations}

Our experiment setup was subject to multiple technological and time-based limitations which would impact the reliability of the results. 

\begin{itemize}
	\item The small number of participants, and the fact that each test was only 
	performed once made most of our data unreliable and susceptible to noise and outliers.
	\item The current setup limits the free movement of the participants which can be uncomfortable. This may alter the typing behaviour of the participant.
	\item The extent to which hardware latency was present in our setup was not estimated nor account for.
		This would require a thorough measurement of the true visual latency of our setup.
	\item Because we allowed participants to use their own phone, there was a situation 
		one participant whose experiment was interrupted by pop-ups.
		A substitute phone from the researchers was provided, but it was clear that the participant was not used 
		to it which affected their typing behaviour.

	\item The webcam setup was unstable and could be spontaneously dismantled by an inopportune bump the laptop or table.
	\item Participants reported during the experiment that they sometimes heavily relied on their muscle memory for typing,
		instead of the visual feed. This feedback could have been included as a question in our qualitative survey or otherwise mitigated.
\end{itemize}
