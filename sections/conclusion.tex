\section{Future Work}
This study was subject to a large number of limitations, and future work should focus on eliminating or mitigating these first. Most prominently, this was a pilot run with three participants, whereas our power analysis suggests a sample size of least 14 for adequate statistical power. Such a study should also be more careful to control confounding variables such as the environment, unstable equipment, hardware latency, and comfort of the participants. 

Typing on a mobile phone is a routine activity for most people nowadays which presents the issue of muscle memory. Our study did not eliminate muscle memory though we did try to equalize it across samples by allowing participants to use their own phones. However, a study which investigates the effect of latency in a condition where muscle memory is impaired, for example by using a randomized keyboard, could provide insights into the effect of latency on coordination tasks this study could not.

Further work beyond eliminating the clear limitations of the presented study could focus on investigating variable latency or other defects in video transmission, such as compression and their effects on coordination tasks. The task could also be modified to a more directly coordination-based task. 

Latency has been investigated extensively in the context of virtual reality, and is known to cause "cybersickness" \cite{stauffert2020latency}. A future study could compare the low-presence condition presented in this study to a high-presence achieved by virtual reality passthrough with latency. 
\section{Conclusion}
We investigated the effect of visual latency on the performance and experience of a smartphone typing task. To test our experiment design, we conducted a pilot study with 3 participants. Our data was too minimal to extract meaningful conclusions, but we were able to identify numerous limitations present in our initial plan.
